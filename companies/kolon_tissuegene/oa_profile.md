# Kolon TissueGene, Inc. — OA Pipeline Profile

*Lighter-touch pull. Last researched 2026-08-21. Not an SEC reporting company (see company_profile.md) — tracked via press releases, the parent's Korean stock filings, ClinicalTrials.gov, and news.*

## Asset Overview

- **TG-C (TissueGene-C)**, marketed in Korea as **Invossa** — an allogeneic cell-and-gene therapy for knee osteoarthritis: a mixture of human chondrocytes plus an irradiated cell line engineered to overexpress **TGF-β1**, delivered as a single ultrasound-guided intra-articular injection. Positioned as a potential first-in-class disease-modifying cell/gene therapy for knee OA (KLG grade 2–3).
- **2017:** Approved in South Korea by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) as "Invossa-K Inj." — the country's first approved gene therapy, on the basis that one cell component was cartilage-derived. [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/health/20190528/license-of-kolons-invossa-revoked)
- **March 2019:** During U.S. Phase 3 testing, it was discovered that the "transformed cells" component was actually **HEK293-derived (kidney-derived, GP2-293) cells**, not cartilage cells as represented in the original Korean filing. Internal Kolon TissueGene emails reportedly showed staff knew of this discrepancy as early as March 2017 — four months before the original Korean approval — and flagged it internally the day after approval was granted. [KoreaBiomed](https://www.koreabiomed.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=5834)
- **May 2019:** FDA placed a clinical hold on the U.S. program. **July 2019:** MFDS formally **revoked Invossa's marketing approval** in Korea and referred the matter for criminal prosecution. [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/health/20190528/license-of-kolons-invossa-revoked); [KoreaBiomed](https://www.koreabiomed.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=23323)
- This is one of the more serious documented cases of a cell/gene therapy approval being revoked for a mischaracterized/mislabeled biological component, and it triggered years of litigation in Korea (patient damages suits, criminal charges against Kolon executives) that are still resolving as of mid-2026 (see Recent News).

## Current Status

**Active but at a critical inflection point: the first of two U.S. Phase 3 trials just failed (July 2026); the program's future depends on the second trial's readout, expected October 2026.**

- Kolon TissueGene ran two U.S. pivotal Phase 3 trials for TG-C in knee OA:
  - **ACTiVION-II (NCT03291470)** — 531 randomized subjects, 27 U.S. sites, completed May 2026. **Topline results reported 2026-07-20/21: failed both co-primary endpoints** (change from baseline in VAS pain and WOMAC total score at Month 12) and **no key secondary endpoints were met**. Safety was consistent with prior data, no new safety signals. [GlobeNewswire, 2026-07-20](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/20/3329671/0/en/Kolon-TissueGene-Reports-Topline-ACTiVION-II-Phase-3-Clinical-Trial-Results-for-TissueGene-C-TG-C-Targeting-Osteoarthritis-OA-of-the-Knee.html); [CGTLive](https://www.cgtlive.com/view/cell-therapy-tg-c-misses-primary-endpoints-phase-3-knee-osteoarthritis-trial)
  - **ACTiVION-I (NCT03203330)** — 535 subjects, currently listed "active, not recruiting" on ClinicalTrials.gov with an estimated completion date of 2026-08-18. Topline results are expected **October 2026**; the company says it will decide on next steps (including a possible BLA filing) after this readout. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03203330); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/kolon-tissuegenes-stock-drops-on-phase-iii-knee-osteoarthritis-trial-failure/)
- Per our project's trial-tracking data, a **third, earlier-stage trial (NCT05276011)** is registered — this is actually a **Phase 2 study in early hip OA** (not knee), listed "not yet recruiting" with an estimated 2027-10 start and a ClinicalTrials.gov record last updated 2026-08-20. This is a separate, much earlier-stage indication (hip, not knee) and does not offset the knee-OA Phase 3 setback. [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05276011](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05276011)
- The company's own pipeline page still lists TG-C for knee OA as "Phase III Phase in progress," hip OA as Phase I, and spinal-disc and veterinary-knee applications as preclinical — i.e., the site had not yet been updated to reflect the ACTiVION-II miss as of this pull. [tissuegene.com/product-pipeline](https://www.tissuegene.com/en_US/product-pipeline)
- Market reaction to the July 2026 miss was severe: Kolon TissueGene (KOSDAQ: 950160) stock fell as much as ~30% on the announcement day and further in the following weeks (reported down ~67-86% for July 2026 overall), and shares of parent/affiliate Kolon Life Science and holding company Kolon Corp were also hit, reflecting how central this asset is to the Kolon group's biotech valuation. [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/08/02/70-percent-of-korean-stocks-fall-in-july-kolon-tissuegene); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/df0895e8-210a-4a39-813b-75ba04c980e1)
- Analysts quoted in coverage attribute the miss less to lack of biological effect and more to an unusually strong placebo response — citing the "joint lavage" effect of intra-articular injection itself, rising GLP-1 use possibly affecting weight/pain in the trial population, and post-pandemic activity-level shifts — and note that a BLA could still be possible under relaxed FDA standards if ACTiVION-I succeeds. [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/kolon-tissuegenes-stock-drops-on-phase-iii-knee-osteoarthritis-trial-failure/)

### Relationship with parent Kolon Life Science

- Kolon TissueGene, Inc. is the U.S. subsidiary that runs the American clinical program; **Kolon Life Science** (the Korean parent, part of Kolon Group) holds the original Korean Invossa marketing history and license. Kolon TissueGene has an active license agreement covering Invossa/TG-C with Kolon Life Science, which the two companies have amended/expanded over time. [tissuegene.com investor PR](https://www.tissuegene.com/en_US/investors/pr/detail/17/kolon-tissuegene-to-expand-license-agreement-for-invossa)
- Kolon TissueGene itself is separately listed on the Korean **KOSDAQ under ticker 950160** (it completed its own offshore IPO/listing in November 2017), so it has its own investor-facing disclosures distinct from Kolon Life Science's. [Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/kolon-tissue-gene-KOSDAQ:950160/)
- Litigation stemming from the 2019 revocation is still working through Korean courts in 2026: on **2026-07-29** the Seoul Central District Court ordered Kolon Group Honorary Chairman **Lee Woong-yeul**, **Kolon Life Science**, **Kolon TissueGene**, and former CEO **Lee Woo-suk** to jointly pay damages (~30M KRW per surviving patient, 50M KRW per deceased patient, ~664M KRW total) to 18 plaintiffs (15 patients dosed 2018-2019 plus 3 bereaved families), finding the defendants "manufactured and sold Invossa despite being able to recognize that it had defects." A separate Korean Supreme Court case over the cancelled marketing authorization itself remains pending. [Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-07-29](https://en.sedaily.com/society/2026/07/29/court-orders-kolon-chairman-lee-to-jointly-compensate)

## Key Scientific Literature

- **Tran H, Tauro W, Mobasheri A, Noh MJ.** "TissueGene-C induces anti-inflammatory activity and M2 macrophage polarization via activation of prostaglandin E2 signaling." *Cytotherapy*, 2025 Mar;27(3):324-337. PMID: [39665739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39665739/). Kolon TissueGene-authored mechanism-of-action study identifying PGE2 (from the chondrocyte component, modulated by TGF-β1) as the key anti-inflammatory mediator; uses a THP-1 macrophage polarization model.
- **Park EH, Seo J, Lee Y, et al.** "TissueGene-C induces long-term analgesic effects through regulation of pain mediators and neuronal sensitization in a rat monoiodoacetate-induced model of osteoarthritis pain." *Osteoarthritis and Cartilage*, 2023 Dec;31(12):1567-1580. PMID: [37544583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37544583/). Preclinical rat-model study (most authors are Kolon Life Science employees) showing TG-C suppresses NGF/CGRP and normalizes neuronal hyperexcitability in dorsal root ganglia — mechanistic support for the analgesic claims tested (and missed) in ACTiVION-II.
- **Brandt MD, Malone JB, Kean TJ.** "Advances and Challenges in the Pursuit of Disease-Modifying Osteoarthritis Drugs: A Review of 2010-2024 Clinical Trials." *Biomedicines*, 2025 Feb 4;13(2):355. PMID: [40002768](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40002768/). Independent review covering TissueGene-C alongside 10 other DMOAD candidates (including Unity's UBX0101) through mid-2024, i.e., before the 2026 Phase 3 readouts.
- Additional PubMed hits (not deep-dived): PMID [37077668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37077668/) (2023, gene therapy targets scoping review), PMID [32696209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32696209/) (2020, rat MIA model anti-inflammatory study), PMID [31884529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31884529/) (2020, cell/gene therapy production platforms review).
- Invossa/mislabeling-adjacent: PMID [28521800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28521800/) (2017, immunogenicity of the "hChonJ" human chondrocyte line used in Invossa — predates the mislabeling discovery).
