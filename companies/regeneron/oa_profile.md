# Regeneron Pharmaceuticals — OA Pipeline Profile (Light-Touch Pull)

*Pulled 2026-08-21. Web + PubMed sources only; no SEC filing deep-dive this pass.*

## Asset Overview

- **Fasinumab** (formerly REGN475) — a fully human monoclonal antibody against nerve growth factor (NGF), created with Regeneron's VelocImmune platform. Same drug class as Pfizer's tanezumab and J&J/Amgen's fulranumab.
- Studied for osteoarthritis (OA) pain of the knee/hip and for chronic low back pain.
- Co-developed globally with **Teva Pharmaceutical Industries** under a collaboration agreement; **Mitsubishi Tanabe Pharma** held exclusive development/commercial rights for Japan and 10 other Asian countries.
- Reached **Phase 3** in OA before the program was discontinued (see Current Status).

## Current Status

**Discontinued — no active clinical development as of this pull (2026-08-21).**

- Regeneron halted the higher-dose treatment arms of the Phase 3 OA program in 2019–2021 because of joint-safety toxicity (rapidly progressive OA requiring joint replacement), consistent with the FDA's broader safety concerns about the anti-NGF antibody class. ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/regeneron-halts-high-doses-phase-3-osteoarthritis-drug-fasinumab-amid-risk-benefit-concerns))
- Regeneron confirmed it was **not advancing fasinumab further** around its Q3 2022 reporting (~November 2022) — described in trade press as Regeneron "canning its last late-stage NGF drug," closing out the entire anti-NGF drug class (Pfizer/Lilly's tanezumab and J&J/Amgen's fulranumab had already been abandoned earlier). ([pharmaphorum, Nov 3 2022](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/and-then-there-were-none-regeneron-cans-last-late-stage-ngf-drug))
- No subsequent news, licensing-out, or partner (Teva/Mitsubishi Tanabe) revival of the program was found in this pull.
- **Note:** despite the 2022 discontinuation, Regeneron/Teva have continued to **publish already-completed Phase 3 trial data** in 2024–2026 (see literature below) — this reflects reporting-out of legacy trial data, not renewed clinical activity.

## Recent News

- **Nov 2022** — Regeneron discontinues further development of fasinumab following the 2018 positive topline Phase 3 efficacy readout and the subsequent high-dose safety halt; ends the anti-NGF drug class industry-wide. ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/and-then-there-were-none-regeneron-cans-last-late-stage-ngf-drug))
- **Oct 2024** — Long-term (52-week) Phase 3 safety/efficacy data published in *Osteoarthritis and Cartilage Open*, based on a 5,331-patient trial: fasinumab 1 mg showed statistically significant WOMAC pain/function improvement at week 16 vs. placebo, but joint-related adverse events (adjudicated arthropathies, joint replacements) occurred at dose-dependent rates. ([PMC11566341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11566341/))
- **Feb 2025** — NSAID-controlled Phase 3 data (1,650 patients) published in *BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders*: fasinumab beat placebo on WOMAC pain/function and was numerically better than NSAIDs, but adjudicated arthropathy rate was 5.6% (fasinumab) vs. 1.5–1.6% (placebo/NSAID). ([PMC11853225](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11853225/))
- **Jan 2026** — Placebo- and naproxen-controlled Phase 3 data published in *RMD Open*: fasinumab 1 mg Q4W beat both placebo and naproxen on WOMAC pain/function at week 16, but arthropathy rates were 7.2–9.7% (fasinumab) vs. 1.1–2.6% (placebo/naproxen), and joint replacement rates ran ~2x placebo. ([PMC12815151](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815151/))
- **Jul 2026** — Biomarker/mechanistic follow-up paper on mineralization and inflammation signals associated with fasinumab treatment, published in *Osteoarthritis and Cartilage*. ([PMID 42436006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42436006/))

These are all publications of previously-completed trials from the discontinued program, not evidence of a revived pipeline.
