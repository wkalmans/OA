# Johnson & Johnson — OA Pipeline Profile (Light-Touch Pull)

*Pulled 2026-08-21. Web + PubMed sources only; no SEC filing deep-dive this pass.*

## Asset Overview

- **JNJ-42160443 = fulranumab** — confirmed. ClinicalTrials.gov lists the same osteoarthritis trials (e.g. NCT00973141, "A Dose-ranging Study of the Safety and Effectiveness of JNJ-42160443 as add-on Treatment in Patients With Osteoarthritis-related Pain") under the internal code JNJ-42160443, while the peer-reviewed literature and later trial registrations for the same program use the INN "fulranumab" — these are the same molecule at different naming stages (code name pre-INN assignment, then INN). ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00973141](https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00973141); [Wikipedia: Fulranumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulranumab))
- Fulranumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody against nerve growth factor (NGF) — same drug class as Regeneron/Teva's fasinumab and Pfizer/Lilly's tanezumab.
- Originally developed by **Amgen**; Johnson & Johnson (via Janssen) **in-licensed it from Amgen in 2008** in a deal reportedly worth ~$425M. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulranumab))
- Studied in OA of the hip/knee (Phase 2 and Phase 3), chronic low back pain, and interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome.

## Current Status

**Discontinued since 2016 — no active fulranumab program at J&J.**

- **March 31, 2016** — Janssen Research & Development announced discontinuation of the fulranumab Phase 3 OA program. J&J stated the decision was based on "strategic portfolio prioritization" and explicitly **not** driven by new safety signals from the Phase 3 studies (though the FDA had placed the whole anti-NGF class, including fulranumab, on partial clinical hold in 2010–2011 over joint-safety concerns before later lifting it). Janssen simultaneously **terminated its 2008 licensing agreement with Amgen**, returning all fulranumab rights to Amgen. ([J&J press release, Mar 31 2016](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/janssen-announces-discontinuation-of-fulranumab-phase-3-development-program-in-osteoarthritis-pain))
- Four Phase 3 OA studies (PAI3001/3002/3003/3007) were terminated early as a result; only ~245 of the planned patients had been enrolled at termination. ([Kelly et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31387410/))
- No indication found that J&J or Amgen has revived fulranumab; it appears to have exited J&J's pipeline entirely for the past ~10 years.

**Beyond fulranumab, J&J does not currently appear to have an active novel OA drug candidate in its own pipeline** as of this pull:
- The J&J investor pipeline page (interactive tool, not fully scrapeable) returned no osteoarthritis/OA/NGF hits in this pass — recommend a follow-up manual check of the interactive filter at investor.jnj.com/pipeline for a fuller confirmation. ([J&J Development Pipeline](https://www.investor.jnj.com/pipeline/development-pipeline/default.aspx))
- The one OA-relevant asset found in 2025–2026 news, **PIPE-791**, is a Contineum Therapeutics (NASDAQ: CTNM) compound — an oral LPA1 receptor antagonist that reported positive Phase 1b topline data in chronic OA pain / chronic low back pain in April 2026. This is **Contineum's own asset, not a J&J-owned or J&J-partnered program** based on available sources; J&J's licensing relationship with Contineum (via Janssen) covers a *different* molecule, PIPE-307 (for a non-OA indication). This should be treated as a false lead for J&J's OA pipeline, not verified J&J activity — flagging for correction if evidence of a J&J license on PIPE-791 specifically turns up later. ([Contineum press release, Apr 2026](https://ir.contineum-tx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/contineum-therapeutics-reports-positive-topline-data-its))
- J&J's actual current OA-related commercial activity is on the **device/injectable side, not a novel drug**: on **July 22, 2025**, Johnson & Johnson MedTech announced a strategic co-promotion agreement with Pacira BioSciences to expand marketing of **ZILRETTA** (triamcinolone acetonide extended-release injectable suspension, an already-FDA-approved corticosteroid, not a novel J&J asset) for knee OA pain — J&J's early-intervention sales force will co-promote the Pacira-owned product to a broader physician base (orthopedics, sports medicine, pain management, rheumatology). This is a commercial/promotional deal, not a J&J pipeline drug. ([J&J press release, Jul 22 2025](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-announces-strategic-agreement-with-pacira-biosciences-to-expand-early-intervention-offering-for-osteoarthritis-of-the-knee); [Pacira press release](https://investor.pacira.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pacira-biosciences-announces-strategic-collaboration))

## Key Scientific Literature (PubMed)

1. **PMID 31387410** — Kelly KM, Sanga P, Zaki N, et al. "Safety and efficacy of fulranumab in osteoarthritis of the hip and knee: results from four early terminated phase III randomized studies." *Current Medical Research and Opinion*, Dec 2019. Of 245 ITT patients (median age 64, 62% women), only 34% completed the double-blind phase (57% of discontinuations were due to early study termination, not AEs). TEAE rates were similar across fulranumab 1mg/3mg and placebo (~57%); no new neurological safety signals. Efficacy trend favored fulranumab on WOMAC pain/function but the sample was too small (due to early termination) for definitive claims. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31387410/
2. **PMID 39998601** — Kou H, et al. "Standardized efficacy and safety evaluation of fulranumab for osteoarthritis." *Clinical Rheumatology*, Apr 2025 (retrospective/meta-analytic re-evaluation of the historical trial data). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39998601/
3. **PMID 41880116** — Abouelella A, et al. "Efficacy and Safety of Anti-nerve Growth Factor Monoclonal Antibodies in Managing Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain." *Drugs*, May 2026 (class-wide review including fulranumab, fasinumab, tanezumab). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41880116/
4. **PMID 33293475** — Cao Z, et al. "Targeting nerve growth factor, a new option for treatment of osteoarthritis: a network meta-analysis." *Aging*, Dec 2020. Cross-drug network meta-analysis including fulranumab among anti-NGF agents. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33293475/
5. Original Phase 2 dose-ranging efficacy paper: Sanga P, et al. "Efficacy, safety, and tolerability of fulranumab, an anti-nerve growth factor antibody, in treatment of patients with moderate-to-severe osteoarthritis pain." *The Journal of Pain*, 2013 (foundational trial for JNJ-42160443/fulranumab in OA; full text access was blocked (403) during this pull — flagged for a follow-up direct read). https://www.jpain.org/article/S1526-5900(11)00263-X/fulltext
