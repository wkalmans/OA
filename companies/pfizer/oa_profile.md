# Pfizer — Osteoarthritis Asset Profile: Tanezumab

**Last updated:** 2026-08-21
**Prepared as part of:** OA drug-development "said vs. did" tracker

---

## Asset Overview

**Tanezumab** (Pfizer code PF-04383119) is a humanized monoclonal antibody that
inhibits **nerve growth factor (NGF)**, a signaling protein involved in pain
sensitization. It was developed as a non-opioid, non-NSAID treatment for
chronic pain from moderate-to-severe osteoarthritis (OA) of the hip or knee,
administered as a subcutaneous injection (2.5 mg or 5 mg, every 8 weeks).
Tanezumab was also studied for cancer pain and chronic low back pain, but OA
was always the lead indication and by far the largest program.

**Development history in brief:**
- Originally developed by Pfizer alone; later co-developed with **Eli Lilly
  and Company** under a collaboration (equal cost/profit-sharing in the U.S.,
  Pfizer-led ex-U.S. with tiered royalties to Lilly).
- **June 2010:** Pfizer suspended the entire OA clinical program worldwide at
  the FDA's request after reports of accelerated joint deterioration leading
  to joint replacement in OA patients. Non-OA tanezumab trials (cancer pain,
  interstitial cystitis, back pain, diabetic peripheral neuropathy) continued.
- **2015:** OA trials resumed under a revised protocol with added imaging-based
  joint-safety surveillance, after the wider anti-NGF drug class (which also
  includes Regeneron/Teva's fasinumab and AstraZeneca/Amgen's fulranumab) went
  through a similar FDA-mandated pause-and-resume.
- **2018–2019:** Phase 3 program (six trials, ~7,000 patients across
  indications) reported topline results.
- **2020–2021:** FDA and EMA regulatory review, culminating in a negative FDA
  Advisory Committee vote and ultimately a Complete Response Letter.
- **October 2021:** Pfizer and Lilly discontinued the global tanezumab
  development program entirely.
- Tanezumab has not appeared in Pfizer's SEC pipeline disclosures (10-K
  narrative text) in any year since — confirmed absent from the FY2021,
  FY2022, FY2024, and FY2025 10-Ks (see Discrepancies section).

This asset is historically significant as **the largest OA pain
drug-development program in the industry** and the clearest public test case
of the anti-NGF mechanism's benefit/risk tradeoff — its outcome effectively
closed the door on the entire anti-NGF class for OA in the U.S.

## Timeline of Public Statements ("Said")

- **2010-06-22** — Pfizer announces suspension of the tanezumab OA program at
  FDA's request, citing a small number of reports of OA patients experiencing
  worsening OA leading to joint replacement. States it will work with FDA "to
  determine the appropriate course of action." Non-OA tanezumab programs
  continue.
  Source: [Pfizer press release, "Pfizer Suspends Tanezumab Osteoarthritis Clinical Trial Program"](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_suspends_tanezumab_osteoarthritis_clinical_trial_program)

- **2018-07-18** — Pfizer/Lilly announce positive topline results from Phase 3
  study A4091056 (16-week, 698 patients, knee/hip OA): both tanezumab arms
  (2.5 mg + 2.5 mg, and 2.5 mg + 5 mg) hit all three co-primary endpoints
  (pain, function, patient global assessment) vs. placebo. RPOA (rapidly
  progressive osteoarthritis) reported in <1.5% of treated patients, none in
  placebo; no osteonecrosis. Company states it will "continue to advance
  tanezumab in the ongoing global Phase 3 program" (six studies, ~7,000
  patients) but does not commit to a filing timeline.
  Source: [Pfizer press release, Phase 3 A4091056 topline results](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_and_lilly_announce_positive_top_line_results_from_phase_3_trial_of_tanezumab_for_the_treatment_of_osteoarthritis_oa_pain)

- **2019-01-29** — Pfizer/Lilly announce topline results from Phase 3 study
  A4091057 (Europe/Japan, 24 weeks): the 5 mg dose met all three co-primary
  endpoints; the 2.5 mg dose met 2 of 3. RPOA in 2.1% of tanezumab patients
  vs. 0% placebo; one osteonecrosis and one subchondral insufficiency
  fracture in treated patients. States detailed results will go to a future
  medical congress.
  Source: [Pfizer press release, Phase 3 A4091057 topline results](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_and_lilly_announce_top_line_results_from_second_phase_3_study_of_tanezumab_in_osteoarthritis_pain)

- **2019-04-18** — Pfizer/Lilly announce topline results from the long-term
  Phase 3 study A4091058 (56-week treatment + 24-week follow-up, ~3,021
  patients, tanezumab vs. NSAIDs). 5 mg arm met 2 of 3 co-primary endpoints at
  week 16 vs. NSAIDs; 2.5 mg arm not significant. Joint-safety composite
  endpoint at 80 weeks: 7.1% (5 mg), 3.8% (2.5 mg), 1.5% (NSAID); total joint
  replacement rates 8.0%, 5.3%, 2.6% respectively. Company states it will
  "review the totality of data... with regulatory authorities."
  Source: [Pfizer press release, long-term Phase 3 A4091058 topline results](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_and_lilly_announce_top_line_results_from_long_term_phase_3_study_of_tanezumab_in_patients_with_osteoarthritis)

- **2020-03-02** — FDA accepts the Biologics License Application (BLA) for
  tanezumab 2.5 mg SC for chronic pain due to moderate-to-severe OA in
  patients with inadequate relief from other analgesics. **Stated PDUFA
  (target decision) date: December 2020.** FDA indicates it plans an Advisory
  Committee meeting. Submission includes 39 Phase 1–3 studies, >18,000
  patients (all indications).
  Source: [Pfizer press release, FDA BLA acceptance](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/u_s_fda_accepts_regulatory_submission_for_tanezumab_a_potential_first_in_class_treatment_for_patients_with_chronic_pain_due_to_moderate_to_severe_osteoarthritis)

- **2021-02-25 (FY2020 10-K, filed)** — Pfizer's pipeline table lists
  tanezumab as "Filed" with FDA (March 2020), EU (March 2020), and Japan
  (Aug. 2020) for "Chronic pain due to moderate-to-severe osteoarthritis";
  also lists tanezumab separately for "Cancer pain" as an earlier-stage
  candidate. Confirms the Lilly collaboration terms (shared U.S.
  costs/profits, Pfizer-led ex-U.S. with royalties to Lilly).
  Source: [Pfizer FY2020 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800321000038/pfe-20201231.htm) (SEC EDGAR, accession 0000078003-21-000038)

- **2021-03-24/25** — FDA's Joint Arthritis Advisory Committee and Drug
  Safety and Risk Management Advisory Committee meet and vote **1 in favor,
  19 against** on whether tanezumab's proposed Risk Evaluation and
  Mitigation Strategy (REMS) would ensure benefits outweigh risks. Pfizer's
  Ken Verburg states: "We will continue to work with the FDA as the agency
  continues its review of our application" — the company maintains it
  believes in tanezumab's benefit-risk profile despite the vote.
  Sources: [Pfizer press release, AdComm vote outcome](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/joint-fda-advisory-committee-votes-application-tanezumab); [Healio coverage, "FDA panel rejects Pfizer's tanezumab..." (2021-03-25)](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20210325/fda-panel-rejects-pfizers-tanezumab-for-osteoarthritis-pain-over-riskbenefit-doubts)

- **2021-05-04/05-13** — Pfizer's Q1 2021 earnings release (8-K Ex-99.1) and
  the Q1 2021 10-Q both restate the March 2021 AdComm 19:1 vote against the
  REMS adequacy, and confirm tanezumab remained "Filed" status with FDA/EU/
  Japan as of the quarter, pending the FDA's continued review — no change in
  Pfizer's public posture on the program's viability yet.
  Sources: [Pfizer Q1 2021 earnings release (Ex-99.1 to 8-K)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800321000062/pfe-04042021xex99.htm); [Pfizer Q1 2021 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800321000065/pfe-20210404.htm) (accession 0000078003-21-000065)

## Actual Outcomes ("Did")

- **2010-06-22** — OA program suspended worldwide by FDA request after joint
  safety reports (see above — this is both a "said" and a "did" entry, as it
  was the regulator forcing Pfizer's hand).

- **2015** — OA trials resume under revised, imaging-based joint-safety
  monitoring protocol (per subsequent literature; exact Pfizer announcement
  date not independently verified in this pass — flagged for future
  confirmation).
  Source: [Roemer et al., "Role of imaging for eligibility and safety of a-NGF clinical trials," Ther Adv Musculoskelet Dis 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37284331/) (PMID 37284331)

- **2018–2019** — All three Phase 3 OA studies (A4091056, A4091057, A4091058)
  read out with a consistent pattern: tanezumab beat placebo/NSAIDs on pain
  and function, but showed a **dose-dependent, statistically clear excess of
  joint safety events** (RPOA, osteonecrosis, subchondral insufficiency
  fracture, and total joint replacement) that grew worse at the higher 5 mg
  dose and over longer follow-up (80-week data showed the largest gap: 7.1%
  vs. 1.5% composite joint safety events, tanezumab 5mg vs. NSAID).

- **2020-03-02** — FDA accepts BLA; PDUFA goal date was December 2020 as
  stated by Pfizer. **This date was not met** — the FDA instead proceeded to
  convene an Advisory Committee, extending the timeline into 2021.

- **2021-03-24/25** — FDA Joint Advisory Committee votes 19–1 against finding
  that tanezumab's proposed REMS would make benefits outweigh risks. FDA
  reasoning (per its own briefing documents): the proposed REMS was "not
  sufficient to mitigate the risk of RPOA," and RPOA incidence was 2–4x
  higher in tanezumab patients vs. NSAID comparators; per FDA briefing
  materials, 15% of RPOA1 cases and 60% of RPOA2 cases progressed to total
  joint replacement.
  Sources: [FDA briefing document, FDA-2021-N-0134](https://www.fda.gov/media/146862/download); [pharmaceutical-technology.com, "FDA panel votes against Pfizer's tanezumab for osteoarthritis pain"](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/fda-pfizer-tanezumab-safety/)

- **2021-09 (September 2021)** — European Medicines Agency's CHMP (Committee
  for Medicinal Products for Human Use) adopts a **negative opinion** on the
  tanezumab OA marketing authorization application, recommending against EU
  approval.
  Source: [Pfizer Q3 2021 earnings release (Ex-99.1 to 8-K, filed 2021-11-02)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800321000104/pfe-10032021xex99.htm)

- **2021 (fall)** — FDA issues a **Complete Response Letter (CRL)** for the
  tanezumab OA BLA (exact CRL issuance date not publicly disclosed by Pfizer
  in the sources reviewed; discontinuation announcement followed within
  weeks).

- **2021-10-26/27** — Pfizer and Eli Lilly jointly announce **discontinuation
  of the entire global clinical development program for tanezumab**, citing
  the FDA CRL and the EMA CHMP negative opinion. This ends the OA indication
  (the program's central goal) — no resubmission, appeal, or partner
  hand-off was announced.
  Sources: [Pfizer Q3 2021 earnings release, Ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800321000104/pfe-10032021xex99.htm); [BioSpace, "Eli Lilly and Pfizer Put Once-Promising Osteoarthritis Pain Drug Tanezumab Out of its Misery" (2021-10-27)](https://www.biospace.com/eli-lilly-and-pfizer-put-once-promising-osteoarthritis-pain-drug-out-of-its-misery)

- **2022-02-24 (FY2021 10-K, filed)** — Tanezumab and osteoarthritis are
  **absent entirely** from the narrative text of Pfizer's FY2021 annual
  report (verified: zero mentions of either term in the full document text).
  The only "Lilly" mentions are a glossary abbreviation entry and a stock
  performance peer-group listing — no discussion of the discontinued
  program, no stated write-off, no retrospective commentary.
  Source: [Pfizer FY2021 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800322000027/pfe-20211231.htm) (accession 0000078003-22-000027) — verified by full-text search of the filing

- **2023 to present (FY2022, FY2024, FY2025 10-Ks)** — Tanezumab and
  osteoarthritis continue to be **absent** from Pfizer's 10-K narrative text
  in every subsequent annual report checked. No revival, resubmission,
  out-licensing, or partnership announcement found in SEC filings or news
  through August 2026.
  Sources: [FY2022 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800323000024/pfe-20221231.htm); [FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800325000054/pfe-20241231.htm); [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800326000026/pfe-20251231.htm) — each verified by full-text search

## Discrepancies / Watch Items

- **The 10-K silence is itself the most notable "said vs. did" gap.** Despite
  tanezumab being (at the time) the largest OA pain drug program in the
  industry, a joint FDA Advisory Committee review, a CRL, and a full program
  discontinuation, Pfizer's subsequent 10-Ks (FY2021 onward) do not discuss
  it at all in the narrative business/pipeline sections. This is consistent
  with standard practice (annual reports emphasize current/active pipeline,
  not post-mortems on discontinued programs), but it means **the fullest
  "said" account of what happened lives in the quarterly earnings press
  releases (8-K Ex-99.1 exhibits), not the 10-Ks** — worth remembering for
  future asset write-ups in this project where a 10-K search alone would
  under-represent a failed program.

- **PDUFA date slipped.** Pfizer stated a December 2020 PDUFA goal date at
  BLA acceptance (March 2020); the actual FDA action was pushed into 2021 via
  the Advisory Committee process, and the eventual outcome (CRL) came many
  months past the original target — a real "said" (Dec. 2020 decision) vs.
  "did" (AdComm in March 2021, CRL and discontinuation in fall 2021) gap of
  roughly 10+ months.

- **Confidence expressed at the AdComm did not match the outcome.** Pfizer's
  March 2021 statement ("we will continue to work with the FDA... we
  believe in tanezumab's benefit-risk profile") came immediately after a
  19-1 adverse vote — the company's public tone did not signal that
  discontinuation was likely just ~7 months later.

- **Not verified in this pass, flagged for follow-up:**
  - The exact date Pfizer announced resumption of OA trials in 2015 (only
    found via a secondary literature citation, not a primary Pfizer source).
  - The exact date the FDA issued the CRL (only the discontinuation
    announcement date, Oct 26/27 2021, is confirmed).
  - Any explicit financial write-off or impairment charge Pfizer or Lilly
    took related to tanezumab — not found in the filings/press releases
    reviewed; would require checking Pfizer's R&D expense footnotes in the
    FY2021 10-K financial statements (not the narrative text, which was
    searched here) or Lilly's own disclosures.
  - Whether Eli Lilly's own SEC filings (10-K/8-K) characterize the
    tanezumab wind-down differently — out of scope for this Pfizer-focused
    profile but relevant to a Lilly asset profile if one is built.

- **Watch going forward:** the 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis (Drugs,
  May 2026, PMID 41880116) shows the anti-NGF mechanism is still being
  actively studied across the class (tanezumab, fasinumab, fulranumab data
  reanalyzed), and Pfizer continues to publish pharmacometric/methods papers
  using the old tanezumab trial data as recently as September 2025 (PMID
  40566820) — this is retrospective/methodological work, not evidence of
  a revived program, but worth periodic re-checking in case any company
  attempts a narrower-population or improved-REMS anti-NGF resubmission
  that could indirectly revive interest in tanezumab-class assets.

## Key Scientific Literature

- **Abouelella A, et al.** "Efficacy and Safety of Anti-nerve Growth Factor
  Monoclonal Antibodies in Managing Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain: A
  Systematic Review with Network Meta-analysis." *Drugs*. 2026 May;
  86(5):719-736. Network meta-analysis of 29 studies / 27,747 patients;
  fasinumab and tanezumab showed the largest pain/function improvements but
  also significantly elevated risk of adjudicated arthropathies (RR ~3.8-4.7x
  placebo) and abnormal peripheral sensation (RR ~2-2.5x placebo).
  PMID: [41880116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41880116/)

- **Carrino JA, et al.** "Characterization of adverse joint outcomes in
  patients with osteoarthritis treated with subcutaneous tanezumab."
  *Osteoarthritis Cartilage*. 2023 Dec;31(12):1612-1626. Pooled Pfizer/Lilly
  Phase 3 analysis (4,541 patients): composite joint safety endpoint in 0%
  placebo, 3.2% tanezumab 2.5mg, 6.2% tanezumab 5mg, 1.5% NSAID — clear
  dose-dependent signal; risk highest in patients with more severe baseline
  radiographic OA (KL grade 2-4).
  PMID: [37652258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37652258/)

- **Brown MT, et al.** "Peripheral Nerve Safety of Nerve Growth Factor
  Inhibition by Tanezumab: Pooled Analyses of Phase III Clinical Studies in
  Over 5000 Patients with Osteoarthritis." *Clin Drug Investig*. 2023 Jul;
  43(7):551-563. Found increased abnormal peripheral sensation (paresthesia,
  hypoesthesia) and mononeuropathy vs. placebo, but no signal for
  generalized peripheral neuropathy.
  PMID: [37460782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37460782/)

- **Colloca L, et al.** "Predicting Treatment Responses in Patients With
  Osteoarthritis: Results From Two Phase III Tanezumab Randomized Clinical
  Trials." *Clin Pharmacol Ther*. 2023 Apr;113(4):878-886. Secondary
  analysis of pooled Phase 3 data identifying baseline predictors of placebo
  vs. tanezumab response — framed as informing future OA trial design.
  PMID: [36621827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36621827/)

- **Roemer FW, et al.** "Role of imaging for eligibility and safety of
  a-NGF clinical trials." *Ther Adv Musculoskelet Dis*. 2023 May 29;
  15:1759720X231171768. Review covering the full anti-NGF class history:
  trials suspended in 2010 over accelerated OA progression concerns, resumed
  in 2015 with imaging-based safety mitigation, followed by the 2021 FDA
  AdComm rejection of tanezumab's REMS. Useful as the best single secondary
  source for the pre-2021 timeline.
  PMID: [37284331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37284331/)

- **Boucher M, Marshall S, Gaitonde P.** "Incorporating Multiple Imputation
  Into Tanezumab Dose-Response Modeling of WOMAC Pain CFB Across Six
  Randomized Placebo-Controlled Phase 3 Trials." *CPT Pharmacometrics Syst
  Pharmacol*. 2025 Sep;14(9):1485-1493. Pfizer-authored methods paper
  revisiting the original regulatory-submission dose-response modeling —
  evidence Pfizer is still mining the tanezumab dataset for methodological
  publications years after discontinuation, not a sign of program revival.
  PMID: [40566820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40566820/)

*(15 PMIDs were returned by the PubMed search overall; the six above were
selected as most directly relevant. Full search: 119 total results for
"tanezumab AND osteoarthritis" as of 2026-08-21.)*
